The deck — three cards, on a loop

Your only in-gym decision is which card is next

Every card is five exercises, roughly 30–35 minutes, and ends with arms so you always leave with a pump. Rest 2 minutes on exercise 1, 90 seconds on 2–3, 45–60 seconds on the rest. Log only your hardest set — one number, in the box.

A Push 14 sets · ~33 min
1 Incline dumbbell press 3 × 6–10 Bench around 30°. You already do this one — it is your best chest movement and you never have to touch a barbell.
2 Machine chest press or pec deck 2 × 10–15 Machine on purpose. No setup, no stabilizing, no thinking. Take it close to failure.
3 Seated dumbbell overhead press 3 × 8–12 The one you already do. Keep doing it.
4 Dumbbell lateral raise 3 × 12–20 Light, slow, elbows leading. This is a waist-narrowing exercise disguised as a shoulder exercise.
5 Triceps pushdown or EZ-bar overhead extension 3 × 10–15 Whichever station is free. Do not walk around looking for the other one.

The floor: no time, no motivation, bad day → do the two shaded exercises and leave. It counts. Advance the card.

B Pull 13 sets · ~32 min
1 Lat pulldown 3 × 8–12 Grip slightly wider than shoulders. Drive elbows down and back, let the stretch happen at the top. This is the waist-narrowing lever — treat it as the most important set of the week.
2 Chest-supported row or seated cable row 3 × 8–12 Chest-supported if the machine exists — no lower-back fatigue, no form decisions.
3 Rear-delt fly (machine or dumbbell) 2 × 15–20 Light. Rear delts finish the shoulder from behind and improve how you stand.
4 EZ-bar curl 3 × 8–12 Your bicep bar. No swinging — if your hips move, the weight is picked for your ego.
5 Dumbbell hammer curl, both arms 2 × 10–15 Exactly how you already do them. Hammers build the brachialis, which is what pushes the bicep up.

The floor: pulldown and row only. Two exercises, twelve minutes, still counts.

C Legs + Arms 14 sets · ~32 min
1 Leg press 3 × 8–15 Feet mid-platform, go to about 90° at the knee. First three weeks: 2 sets only, and stop 3 reps short. Soreness is the reason you never came back last time.
2 Hamstring curl machine 3 × 10–15 Hamstrings and glutes pull your pelvis out of a forward tilt. That tilt is making your stomach push forward more than the fat alone does.
3 Leg extension 2 × 12–20 Optional if you are wrecked. Skip it before you skip anything above it.
4 Dumbbell curl + triceps pushdown, back to back 3 × 10–15 Curl a set, pushdown a set, no rest between them. This is your reward for showing up to leg day and it costs six minutes.
5 Cable crunch or lying leg raise 2 × 10–20 Not for fat loss — that is not how it works. This is so there is something worth seeing when the fat does come off.

The floor: leg press and hamstring curl. Then go home. You still trained legs, which is more than the last several years.

How often

Three or four sessions per seven days. Two is a holding pattern that still keeps your muscle. Five is fine but unnecessary. The deck does not care — it only advances when you train.

Why arms are in every card

Partly because arms recover fast and take frequency well. Mostly because arms are the thing that gets you through the door, and a plan you attend beats a better plan you skip.

Progressive overload

Add reps until the top of the range, then add weight. That is the entire system.

It is called double progression, and it exists so that you never have to decide anything in the gym. Every exercise has a rep range. You have one job.

The rule
  • Pick a weight you can do for the bottom of the range with about two reps left in you.
  • Every session, try to add one rep to your top set. Not weight — reps.
  • When you hit the top of the range on every set, go up in weight next session.
  • Your reps will drop when you go up. That is correct. Climb back up the range and repeat forever.

How much to go up

The right jump is roughly 2.5–5% of the load. On small muscles the smallest dumbbell in the rack is already a huge jump, which is why the rep ranges on isolation work are wider.

What a real year looks like

You are natural, you are not a beginner, and you are in a calorie deficit. Here is the honest arithmetic so you stop measuring yourself against a fantasy:

  • Weeks 1–4: numbers climb fast on almost everything. Most of that is skill and confidence with the movement, not new muscle. Enjoy it, do not extrapolate it.
  • Months 2–6, in a deficit: holding your numbers is the progress. If you weigh 18 lb less and still press the same dumbbells for the same reps, you kept your muscle through a cut. That is the win condition.
  • A good year, eating enough: maybe +10–15 lb per hand on a dumbbell press, +5–10 lb on curls, +60–100 lb on a leg press. Those numbers look small written down and they look enormous on a body.
The thing you noticed

Why you got weaker in the gym at the exact moment you started looking better

You described this precisely: you cut, you looked better than you had in years, and your lifts fell off a cliff. That is not you losing muscle. In order of how much it is costing you:

  • Glycogen. Muscle stores carbohydrate, and each gram of it holds about three grams of water. Eat less, store less, and your muscles are literally smaller, flatter and less powerful — without a single fiber being lost.
  • Recovery debt. A deficit slows tissue repair. The same session that used to leave you fine now leaves you three-quarters recovered when the next one arrives, and it compounds.
  • Leverage. You genuinely have less body to press against. This is most obvious on pressing, which is one of several reasons you can stop worrying about the bench press forever.
  • On a GLP-1 specifically: you are eating very little food, which means very little carbohydrate, and — the one almost nobody catches — very little sodium. Low sodium means low blood volume, which means flat pumps, light-headedness on standing, and dead sets.

So the fix is not to push harder. Pushing harder into a recovery deficit is how the last several attempts ended.

What to do instead
  • Change the win condition. While the scale is moving down, your goal is to keep the same numbers, not add to them. Write that down somewhere.
  • Put carbs in front of the session. 30–60 g, 60–90 minutes before. On a GLP-1 your stomach empties slowly, so drink them — juice, a shake with a banana, a sports drink. This one change will noticeably bring your sets back.
  • Salt your food. Deliberately. You are almost certainly under-eating sodium now that you are barely eating.
  • Creatine, 5 g a day, every day. Cheapest, most-studied, fully natural. It preserves performance in a deficit. It will also put 2–4 lb of water inside your muscles — that is a scale increase that means you are winning.
  • Slow down if it does not recover. See the trigger below.
The back-off trigger

If your top set drops 2 or more reps at the same weight, on two sessions in a row, across two or more different exercises — that is not laziness and it is not a bad day. That is your recovery telling you the deficit is too steep. Eat more carbs, sleep, or slow the rate of loss. Do not try to out-discipline it.

Measuring

Your 5-pound daily swing is noise, and it is making you crazy

Sodium, carbohydrate, stress, sleep, whether you have been to the bathroom — a five-pound day-to-day range is completely normal and contains almost no information about fat. Reading it daily is like judging the climate from this afternoon.

Weigh every morning anyway — same time, after the bathroom, before food. But never look at a single number. Look only at the 7-day rolling average, and compare it to last week's average. Any scale app or a spreadsheet column does this for you. One week of average-versus-average is a real data point. One morning is not.

Take a tape measure to your navel once a week. Same morning, standing, normal exhale, no sucking in. For what you actually care about, waist circumference is a far better instrument than bodyweight — it moves when fat moves and it ignores water almost entirely.

On looking better dehydrated: you are right, and you should not chase it. Water under the skin blurs definition, so pulling it out sharpens you for an evening. It is also not a change in your body, it costs you strength, and being chronically under-hydrated will wreck the exact training sessions that are supposed to be building the muscle underneath. Judge the mirror on a normal, well-fed, well-watered day — that is the body you actually have.

Eating on a GLP-1

The drug removes your appetite. It does not remove your protein requirement.

This is the single highest-stakes part of the whole plan. When people lose weight fast without resistance training and without enough protein, a large share of what they lose is lean tissue — commonly a quarter to a third of the total. You are already solving half of that with the deck. The other half is protein, and appetite suppression is actively working against you.

Protein first, at every single meal. Not a rule about health — a rule about capacity. You have a small number of bites available per day now. Spend them on the thing that keeps your arms.

Daily protein target 156–195 g
Which is roughly 3 meals of ~52–65 g

Based on 0.8–1.0 g per pound of your goal weight — the standard target for holding muscle through a deficit. If you can only manage 1,200 calories on a heavy dose day, make 700 of them protein and let the rest fall where it falls.

Where it comes from when nothing sounds good

Shakes are legitimate food, not a supplement — two a day is fine. Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, deli turkey, egg whites, rotisserie chicken. Liquid and cold beat hot and heavy when your stomach is slow.

Rate of loss

Cap it at 1% of bodyweight per week; 0.5–0.75% is the sweet spot for keeping muscle. Retatrutide can easily drive faster than that, and faster is exactly where lean mass goes.

Creatine monohydrate

5 g daily, any time, no loading phase. It is natural, it is the most studied supplement there is, and it does more for you in a deficit than in a surplus.

Cardio

You do not need it for the deficit — the drug is handling that. Walk 8–10k steps because it helps visceral fat, mood and focus. Do not add hard cardio that eats the recovery your lifting needs.

The honest part

About the stomach

There is no exercise that removes fat from where you do the exercise. Crunches do not burn stomach fat. If they did, gum would be a jaw treatment. Abdominal work builds abdominal muscle, which is worth doing, but it is not a fat loss tool and every year you spend hoping otherwise is a year lost.

Your order of operations is genetic and you do not get to pick it. You described both parents as carrying weight centrally. That is your map: your body will clear the face, then the arms, then the legs and chest, and it will defend the abdomen until near the end. When you were lean enough to see arm definition, your stomach was always going to still be there. It is not evidence that anything failed.

What is actually working in your favor right now. The class of drug you are on is unusually effective on visceral fat — the deep abdominal fat that sits behind the wall and pushes it forward. That specific fat often comes off ahead of the subcutaneous layer, which means the shape of your midsection can improve before the pinch test does.

And what you control. Three things move the look of your waist, in this order of speed:

  • Lats and side delts — contrast. Card B exercise 1 and Card A exercise 4. Fastest visible change available to you.
  • Posture — hamstrings and glutes pull your pelvis back under you and your ribs down. Card C exercise 2. A forward pelvic tilt adds visual inches to a stomach that is not actually that big.
  • Continued fat loss — slowest, but it is the one that finishes the job. From where you are, abs are likely another 15–25 lb. The last ten will take as long as the first thirty. That is normal and it is not a sign to change plans.
Maintenance

Backing off on purpose, before your body does it for you

Every 6–8 weeks, or on the trigger

Run one week of the same cards, the same weights, half the sets. Do not take the week off entirely — for you, stopping is the risk. Keep the habit, drop the load.

Expect to need it more often

In an aggressive deficit on a GLP-1, recovery is worse than normal. If you feel like you need a light week at 5 weeks, take it at 5 weeks. Nothing in this plan is a schedule.

Why the volume is deliberately modest

Roughly 10–14 hard sets per muscle per week is where muscle retention and slow growth live. More is not better while under-fed — it is just more fatigue. Your short-session preference and the optimal dose happen to agree.

When you come back after a gap

Two weeks off? Take 10% off your logged numbers for one session, then resume. Two months off? Take 20% off for two sessions. Then you are back. No restart, no penance.
